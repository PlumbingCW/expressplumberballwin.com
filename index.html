Ballwin homes and the plumbing they have

Ballwin incorporated in 1950, and census counts show its population doubling between 1960 and 1970 and again between 1980 and 1990. That means plenty of houses from several building eras, each with its own pipe materials and water heater habits. Tell the dispatcher the age of your house and we plan around it.

Many homes here have basements, which is where water heaters, sump pits, floor drains and main shutoffs live. A basement is also where a plumbing failure does the most damage, so know where your main valve is before you need it.

Hard water, sewers and the rules that apply here

Missouri American Water's 2023 report for the St. Louis County system lists total hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate. That reaches into hard water, so scale in water heaters and on fixtures is a common reason for calls. Our water softener page explains the options.

According to the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District, owners are responsible for the entire private building sewer. The City of Ballwin also runs a Sewer Lateral Repair Program with a $4,500 per-job cap and a $150 application fee, which can help with repeat root problems. Details are on the sewer line repair page.

For permits, call the Ballwin Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129. The city says contractors must hold a Ballwin license and an active St. Louis County license in their trade, and that finishing a basement requires a permit.
