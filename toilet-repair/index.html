Toilet overflowing: stop the water in under a minute

Do not flush again. Remove the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the refill, or turn the oval handle on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. Mop up water at once, because bathroom floors and the ceiling below can absorb a lot.

If the toilet is overflowing and other drains are slow, the problem is not the toilet alone. A blocked branch or main line is the likely cause, which the sewer line cleaning page covers.

A running toilet can waste a surprising amount of water without any visible leak. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes, and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

Hard water and toilet parts

Missouri American Water reports total hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate in its 2023 St. Louis County report. Mineral deposits clog the small rim jets under the bowl lip and make flappers stiff and leaky. Cleaning the jets with a descaler restores a weak flush, and a water softener slows the buildup.

Older toilets, especially those from before the 1990s, may use more water per flush. Replacing one is a decision for later. First we tell you whether a repair costs less than a new toilet.

When to call a plumber: signs the problem is deeper than the toilet

Signs the problem is deeper than the toilet

  • ✓Gurgling in the tub or sink when the toilet flushes
  • ✓Water rising in a basement floor drain after a flush
  • ✓Several toilets slow at once
  • ✓Sewage odor with no visible cause
  • ✓A toilet that clogs again within a day of clearing

Any of these signals a line problem. Call the dispatcher and mention it, so the plumber brings the cable or camera.
