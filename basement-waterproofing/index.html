Find the source before you buy a solution

Water gets into a basement in a handful of ways. It rises through the floor, seeps through the wall, runs in at a crack or along the joint where the wall meets the floor, or comes from a plumbing leak that only looks like groundwater. A plumber's first job is to sort which.

Where water enters and what it suggests
Where it showsLikely sourceUsual fix
Along the floor wall jointHydrostatic pressureInterior drain and sump
Through a wall crackFoundation movement or pressureCrack repair, exterior drainage
Damp patch below a pipePlumbing leakLeak detection and pipe repair
Floor drain backupSewer lineSewer line cleaning
Window wellPoor grade or clogged drainClean the well, improve grading

A damp spot may be a pipe, not the ground. A leak detection visit rules plumbing in or out before you commit to a larger project.

Common waterproofing systems

An interior drain system runs a perforated channel along the footing at the base of the wall. Water collects there and flows to a sump pit, where a pump sends it outside. It is the most common retrofit because it works from inside and avoids digging around the house.

Exterior systems dig down to the footing, repair and coat the wall, and install new drain tile. They cost more and disturb the yard. Grading and gutter fixes are a cheap first step. Gutter downspouts should discharge several feet from the foundation, and soil near the wall should slope away from it.

Planning around permits and finishing

The City of Ballwin states that plumbing permits are typically needed with basement finishing, and Building Inspections can be reached at (636) 227-2129. If you are planning to finish the basement, solve the water first. Carpet, drywall and insulation hide moisture, and they trap it.

Before you finish a basement

  • ✓Run a dehumidifier for a week and track the humidity
  • ✓Tape a square of plastic to the floor and wall to see if moisture collects
  • ✓Check gutters, downspouts and grading
  • ✓Get a camera look at the sewer lateral
  • ✓Confirm the sump pump runs and has a backup

A sewer camera, described on the sewer inspection page, is a sensible step before finishing, because a lateral problem under the slab causes damage that looks like a waterproofing failure.
