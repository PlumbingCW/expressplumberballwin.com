Sewer backup: what to do before the jetter arrives

When the main line is blocked, every fixture in the house is a possible overflow point. The basement floor drain, a tub or a low toilet is where sewage shows first. Stop running water, keep children and pets out, and avoid touching standing water.

Do not pour chemical cleaners into a backed-up line. They sit in the standing water, they do not clear roots, and they make the job harder to work in. Open windows if there is an odor and wait for the plumber.

How hydro jetting works

A jetter pushes a hose with a nozzle through the pipe and fires pressurized water backward and sideways against the pipe wall. The spray cuts roots, shreds grease and scours the pipe back to its full width. Water pressure and nozzle choice are matched to the pipe material and age.

A cable punches a hole through a clog. A jetter cleans the whole circumference. That difference is why jetting holds longer in lines that clog every few weeks, and why drain cleaning with a cable is tried first on routine blockages.

Roots, grease and the repeat-clog pattern

Tree roots find tiny joints in older clay and cast iron laterals, grow into the pipe, and catch everything that flows past. In a few weeks they form a net that holds wipes, grease and paper. A cable cuts a channel through the net. Jetting removes the root mass.

Ballwin's Sewer Lateral Repair Program, which has a $4,500 cap per job and a $150 application fee, states that roots requiring clearing more than once a year can qualify a homeowner. If jetting becomes an annual ritual, ask a plumber about a repair and keep every invoice.

Why a camera belongs before and after

Jetting at full pressure through cracked or collapsed pipe makes things worse. A short camera run shows whether the pipe can take it. After jetting, a second run confirms the pipe is clean and shows any damage the roots were hiding, which is the basis of a sewer line repair estimate if one is needed.

MSD, the public main and your private line

The Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District says owners are responsible for the entire private building sewer and that about 85 percent of backups are not caused by the public sewer. If multiple neighbors are backed up at once, the public main may be the cause, and MSD takes reports at 1 (866) 281-5737. The usual case is a private line on one property.

Standards note: Sewer work is governed by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for pipe sizing and cleanout access, and a cleanout makes jetting faster. The City of Ballwin lists the International Plumbing Code among its adopted codes, and the Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 can confirm which edition applies.
