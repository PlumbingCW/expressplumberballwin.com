Larger homes, longer pipe runs

Town and Country covers about 11.5 square miles and counted 11,640 residents and 3,842 housing units in 2020. The area was farmland known as Altheim as early as 1886, became a village in 1950 and a fourth class city in 1975. Where a home sits on a large lot, the supply line and the sewer lateral are both longer.

Longer runs change the plumbing job. A leak in a distant wing can waste water for weeks before a bill shows it, and a lateral that travels far to the street has more places to crack. That is why leak detection and water heater replacement lead the calls we receive from here, and why around-the-clock dispatch matters for a house with several bathrooms.

If a pipe bursts or a leak is active, start here

Close the main

Locate it near the meter or where the line enters the basement. In a large house, close the zone valve too if you have one.

Cut power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms and any basement panel that sits near standing water.

Protect finishes

Lift rugs and art from the floor, and stop water from reaching hardwood if you can.

Who to call for permits and rules

The City of Town and Country administers its own building rules. Call the city before a remodel with new plumbing, a water heater relocation or a sewer dig, and send us the answer. Larger renovation projects often tie in a plumbing installation scope.

Sewer problems follow the same ownership rule as the rest of St. Louis County. According to MSD, owners are responsible for the whole private building sewer. If many neighbors back up together, call MSD at 1 (866) 281-5737.

Water quality and fixtures

Missouri American Water's 2023 St. Louis County report lists hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate. If you are on that system, expect scale in tanks and on glass. A water softener reduces buildup and can extend the life of the heaters in a big home.
