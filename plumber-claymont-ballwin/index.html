A ranch subdivision developed around 1963

St. Louis Patina's history of Claymont says the subdivision was developed around 1963, with model-home photographs dated April 1962 and April 1963. The houses were ranches with model names such as Chateau, Somerset, Tremont and Coronado, built with two-car garages, two bathrooms and basements.

The same writer later noted that the houses look much the same as they did when built. That is useful for a plumber to know, because original-era houses are often where a kitchen or bath is updated while the plumbing behind the walls is left alone.

Claymont has a board of trustees and its own association website, and dues are handled through a management company. Check the subdivision's rules before work that changes the outside of the house.

What a ranch with a basement means for a plumbing problem

In a basement ranch, the main shutoff, the water heater and the floor drain often sit on the same lower level. That is convenient when everything works and messy when it does not, because a leaking tank or a backed-up floor drain puts water into a space the family may use. Learn where your main shutoff is before you need it.

Two bathrooms on one level also give you a quick diagnostic. If both slow down together, or a floor drain gurgles when a toilet flushes, the main drain is the likely suspect and a single fixture fix will not help. See drain cleaning and hydro jetting for repeat blockages.

Remodeling a Claymont house: inspect before you cover

Many ranches of this age have been updated room by room. A remodel is the cheapest moment to have old shutoff valves, supply lines and drain connections checked, before new tile or cabinets cover them. You cannot tell the pipe material from the build year, so ask the plumber to show you what is actually there.

If you are replacing a toilet or fixtures, see toilet repair. If the water heater is original to a remodel from decades ago, see water heater replacement.

Know who owns what

According to MSD, the line that leaves your house is yours to maintain and the main in the street is theirs. A blockage that appears in several Claymont houses on the same night points to the main, which MSD takes at 1 (866) 281-5737.

The city's lateral repair program, paid for by a $28 annual charge on tax bills, covers repairs for continuous blockages or defects that cabling cannot cure, up to $4,500 per job after a $150 application. Ask the Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 whether your house qualifies.

Call 811 before any digging, including a new fence post or a sewer repair, so underground lines can be marked.

Hard water and a basement water heater

Hardness in the St. Louis County system ranged from 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate in Missouri American Water's 2023 report. If that utility supplies your address, tanks that have run for many years collect the most scale. Scale builds up fastest where a tank has run for many years.
