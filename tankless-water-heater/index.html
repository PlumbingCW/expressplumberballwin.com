What tankless service covers

A tankless heater heats water as it flows through a heat exchanger, so there is no tank to leak or store sediment. The price of that convenience is scale. Minerals bake onto the heat exchanger, flow drops, and the unit throws an error code or shuts off.

Service falls into three jobs: installing a new unit, repairing a faulty one, and flushing scale out of a working one. The first two are straightforward. The third is the one most owners skip, and it explains most of the failure calls.

Flush and descale: the job people forget

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, usually through service valves on the unit, then rinses it. It takes a couple of hours and brings flow back to normal. Many manufacturers call for a flush every year in hard water areas.

Missouri American Water reports total hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate in its 2023 St. Louis County report, depending on the treatment plant. That is moderately hard to hard water, so annual flushing is a sensible default here. A water softener cuts the scale load further.

Signs your tankless unit needs a flush

  • ✓Hot water output has weakened or turned lukewarm
  • ✓The unit shows a recurring error code about flow or temperature
  • ✓The heater makes a rumbling or popping sound while running
  • ✓Water takes longer to heat than it did when new
  • ✓It has been more than a year since the last flush

Installing or converting to tankless

Gas tankless units need a gas line large enough to feed the burner at full fire, and a vent rated for the unit. Electric models need substantial amperage, and some homes need panel work. If you are switching from a tank, see the water heater installation page for the shared steps.

In a failure situation, many owners get a tank installed to restore hot water quickly and convert later. The replacement page explains that option.

Ballwin households that suit tankless

Tankless fits households that run showers back to back, because the supply never runs out. It is a weaker choice for a home with a very small gas line or for someone who runs several fixtures at once and expects every one at full temperature.
