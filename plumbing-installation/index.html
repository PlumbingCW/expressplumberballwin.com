What we install

Installation work ranges from a single faucet to a whole bathroom. A plumber sets the drain, vent and supply lines first, then the fixtures. Mistakes at the rough-in stage are expensive to fix after the walls close, so the order of work matters.

Basement bathrooms in Ballwin homes

Many Ballwin homes have basements, and finishing one is a common project. A bathroom below the main sewer line may need a sewage ejector pump, because waste cannot drain downhill to the lateral. The pump sits in a sealed basin and lifts waste up to the line.

The City of Ballwin states that plumbing permits are typically needed with basement finishing. Ask the Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 about the process, and confirm whether the project needs an inspection before walls are closed.

Contractor rules in Ballwin

The City of Ballwin says contractors must hold a Ballwin contractor license along with an active St. Louis County license in their trade. Ask any plumber, including us, to confirm their license status with the city before you sign. A permit pulled by an unlicensed contractor can cause trouble when you sell.

Outdoor spigots, gas lines and appliance hookups

Not every installation is inside a bathroom. A frost-proof hose bib has a long stem so the shutoff sits inside the warm wall, which protects the pipe in a hard freeze. Replacing an old sillcock before winter is a small job that avoids a burst pipe later.

Gas appliances such as ranges and dryers need a licensed plumber to run and pressure test the line. Appliance hookups, including dishwashers and ice makers, need a shutoff valve within reach. If a hookup leaks or a stop valve seizes, see faucet repair for how we handle fixtures and valves.
