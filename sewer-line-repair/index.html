Sewage backing up: protect the house first

A broken sewer lateral sends wastewater to the lowest drain in the house. In most Ballwin homes that is a basement floor drain or a basement bathroom. Stop all water use at once, including the washing machine and dishwasher, because every gallon you run adds to the backup.

Keep children and pets away from standing water, since it carries bacteria. Do not use chemical drain openers on a line that is already full. Call the dispatcher, describe where the water appeared, and mention if the line has been cleared before.

How we confirm the break before we dig

A sewer camera runs through a cleanout and shows the pipe from the inside. It reveals a cracked section, a joint that has pulled apart, a belly that holds water, or roots coming through a seam. You see the same video the plumber sees, and the footage is the basis for the quote.

A locator on the camera head marks the break on the surface, so the work is aimed at one spot rather than a long trench. If the camera shows only a heavy clog and sound pipe, the answer may be hydro jetting rather than a dig.

Who owns which part of the sewer line

The Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District states that the owner is responsible for the whole private building sewer, from the house to the public main. MSD also reports that roughly 85 percent of backups are not caused by the public sewer. If several neighbors back up at the same time, the public main may be at fault, and MSD takes reports at 1 (866) 281-5737.

Ballwin runs a Sewer Lateral Repair Program with a $4,500 cap per job and a $150 application fee, and its rules cover problems such as roots that need clearing more than once a year. Read the program terms and apply before work begins. Keep the camera report and every invoice.

Why older Ballwin laterals fail

Census counts show Ballwin's population doubling between 1960 and 1970 and again between 1980 and 1990, so many laterals are decades old. Clay tile and cast iron joints loosen with time, and roots find them. Settling soil can leave a low spot where waste collects and clogs build again.

Check the lateral when you buy a home or before a basement finish. The sewer inspection page explains what a camera visit covers and what the report should show.
