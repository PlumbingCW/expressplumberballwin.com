What is documented about Woodsmill

The association's website carries the line "est. 1968," and a sampled listing for a house on Dutch Mill Drive shows a 1968 build year and a legal description of Woodsmill Plat 1. A guide to Ballwin's HOA communities puts the subdivision at 269 homes, with a clubhouse, common ground and a lake.

That makes Woodsmill a mostly single-era neighborhood, but not a uniform one. Over five decades owners have remodeled kitchens and baths, swapped water heaters and changed fixtures, so the age of the subdivision tells you less than the history of your own house.

Woodsmill sits in the part of Ballwin the city calls East of Henry Road. The city's rezoning page says Woodsmill and Woodsmill Village were among the subdivisions annexed from St. Louis County in 1996. For a Woodsmill address, permit questions start with the City of Ballwin.

Basements, sump pumps and what a build year does not tell you

One sampled Woodsmill listing describes a partially finished basement with a sump pump. A single listing proves nothing about every house, so treat it as a reminder and not a finding. If your house has a basement, test the sump pit each season: pour in a bucket of water and watch the pump start and shut off. A pump that does not run is far cheaper to fix on a dry day. See sump pump repair and basement waterproofing.

A 1968 build year also does not tell you what your pipes are made of. Many houses of that age have had repairs over the decades, and the only honest way to know the condition of the main drain is to look at it. A sewer camera inspection shows the actual pipe instead of guessing from the date.

Before we arrive: if water is spreading

Find the main

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. In a basement house it is usually near where the line enters or by the meter.

Protect the electrical

Switch off breakers for wet rooms and stay out of standing water near the panel.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and take photos before cleanup for your records.

Call with details

Dial (314) 461-6239 and tell us the street, where the water is coming from and what you have shut off.

Who owns what: the sewer line and the city program

MSD says property owners are responsible for the whole private building sewer, meaning the pipe from the house to the public main. When several houses on a street back up together, suspect the public main and call MSD at 1 (866) 281-5737.

Ballwin's Sewer Lateral Repair Program is funded by a $28 yearly fee on the property tax bill and is meant for continuous blockages or structural defects that cabling cannot permanently fix. The application fee is $150 and the cap is $4,500 per job. It is a city program, not a Woodsmill one, so call Inspections at (636) 227-2129 to confirm your address and the current rules.

Hard water and your fixtures

Missouri American Water's 2023 St. Louis County report lists total hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate. If that utility serves your house, scale is a likely cause when a faucet loses flow or a heater gets noisy. If a faucet has lost flow or a heater is noisy, scale is a common cause.
