What a sewer camera inspection shows

A flexible rod carries a camera and light through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and down the lateral toward the street. The plumber watches the screen, notes distance at each defect and records the run. A locator on the camera head lets the crew mark the pipe depth and path on the ground.

The footage shows whether the pipe is sound or has cracks, offset joints, roots, scale, a low spot that holds water, or a collapse. It also confirms the pipe material, which tells you how much life it may have left.

When an inspection is worth booking

  • ✓You are buying a home built before the 1990s
  • ✓Drains in the basement slow or gurgle more than once a year
  • ✓A cable or jetter has cleared the same line twice
  • ✓You plan to finish a basement or add a bathroom
  • ✓Large trees sit between the house and the street

Local programs and rules to know

MSD says the owner is responsible for the entire private building sewer. Ballwin's Sewer Lateral Repair Program lists a $4,500 cap per job and a $150 application fee, and the city states that roots needing clearing more than once a year can be a qualifying problem. A dated camera report supports an application.

Ballwin also has inspection and certification requirements for occupied buildings, according to its Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129. Ask what applies to a sale before you list the house.

Why Ballwin buyers ask for it

Population counts show Ballwin growing fast in the 1960s and again in the 1980s, so many laterals date from those building booms. A pipe that old can still be fine, but a camera is the only way to be sure. Buyers in Town and Country and Ballwin often order one during the inspection window.
