When a clogged drain becomes an emergency

One slow sink is a nuisance. Water rising in a tub when the washing machine drains is a different problem, because it means the blockage sits in the shared line downstream of every fixture. Gurgling toilets, a sewage smell in the basement and water at the lowest floor drain all point to the main drain, and that is when waiting a day costs money.

While you wait for the plumber, stop using every sink, shower, dishwasher and washer. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and in a blocked main it goes to the lowest opening in the house. Skip the bottle of chemical drain opener, since it sits in standing water and can burn a plumber's hands when the line is opened.

Call right away if you notice any of these

  • ✓Water backing up in a tub, shower or floor drain when another fixture is used
  • ✓More than one fixture draining slowly at the same time
  • ✓A toilet that gurgles or bubbles when a sink or washer runs
  • ✓Sewage smell near the basement floor drain or the cleanout cap
  • ✓Water pooling around a floor drain after rain or after laundry

Reading the symptoms to find where the blockage sits

The first few minutes on site are spent working out which pipe is blocked, because cabling the wrong line wastes time. Branch lines serve one fixture or one bathroom. The main drain collects every branch and leaves the house toward the sewer lateral.

Kitchen sink clogs in Ballwin homes

Kitchen drains clog more often than any other line because grease cools and sticks to the pipe wall a little more with every wash. Coffee grounds, rice and fibrous food scraps add to it, even with a disposal. By the time the sink stops draining, the pipe is often narrowed along several feet, not blocked at one spot.

Cabling breaks the plug free, and the cable's cutting head scrapes the wall as it is pulled back. If the line is heavily coated, a hydro jetting pass washes the pipe clean and is usually the better answer when the same sink has been snaked more than once. If the sink is plumbed to a disposal that has jammed, the garbage disposal repair page covers that side of the fix.

Snaking, cabling and when we step up to jetting

A drain snake is a spring-steel cable with a head that bores through a soft clog. A heavier cable machine uses larger heads for roots and hardened buildup in 3 and 4 inch lines. Both are mechanical tools, and both leave some residue on the pipe wall.

Jetting uses high-pressure water to scour that residue away. Plumbers move to it when a cable only opens a small channel, when roots have returned, or when grease is the cause. If the cable stops short, or the line keeps clogging, we recommend a sewer camera inspection so the repair plan is based on what is actually in the pipe.

Repeat clogs and the Ballwin sewer lateral program

A line that clogs again and again is a pipe problem, not a cleaning problem. The City of Ballwin runs a Sewer Lateral Repair Program for homeowners with continuous blockages. According to the city, it is funded by a $28.00 annual fee approved by voters in April 1999, pays up to $4,500 per job, and carries a $150.00 application fee.

The city says root growth that needs clearing more than once a year can qualify, while clearing once a year or less is treated as normal maintenance. That makes paper records valuable. Keep every invoice from every time the line is cleared, because the city asks for proof when cabling could not open the line. The Ballwin Inspections Department can be reached at (636) 227-2129 for the current application.

What to do after the visit to keep the drain open

Grease goes in the trash, not the sink. A mesh strainer in the kitchen sink and a hair catcher in each shower prevent most branch clogs. For a line that has been jetted, a camera follow-up confirms the pipe is sound.

If the main line was the problem, sewer line cleaning on a schedule keeps roots in check, and neighbors in Manchester with older clay or cast iron laterals ask about the same thing. A plumber can tell you whether yours is a candidate.

Standards note: The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires every fixture drain to have a trap and a vent, which is why a blocked vent can mimic a clog. The City of Ballwin lists the International Plumbing Code among its adopted codes, and the Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 can confirm which edition applies.
