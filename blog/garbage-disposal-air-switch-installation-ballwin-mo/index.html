Garbage Disposal Air Switch Installation in Ballwin, MO

How an air switch works, how it is installed and when a plumber or electrician is needed.

An air switch is a button on the countertop that turns the disposal on without a wall switch. Pressing it sends a puff of air through a small tube to a module under the sink, which closes the circuit. There is no electricity at the button itself, which is why many homeowners prefer it to a switch near the sink.

This guide explains how an air switch works, what the install involves and the cases where the job is bigger than it looks. If your disposal is jammed or leaking instead, see the garbage disposal repair page.

What an air switch includes

Parts of a typical air switch kit
PartWhat it doesWhere it goes
Button and capYou press it to start the disposalIn a hole in the countertop or sink deck
Air tubeCarries the pulse of airRuns down through the cabinet
Control moduleCloses the circuit when it feels the airMounted under the sink, plugged into power
Disposal power cordPlugs into the moduleInto the module's outlet

Most kits plug into a standard outlet under the sink and the disposal plugs into the module. That makes the electrical part simple when an outlet already exists. If there is no outlet, or it is a hardwired disposal with no cord, the job calls for an electrician or a licensed plumber.

Step by step: what the installation involves

  1. 01

    Choose the spot

    Pick a location within reach of the sink, away from the faucet splash and with clear space underneath for the tube.

  2. 02

    Drill the hole

    Use the template or size listed in the kit. Drilling through stone or quartz needs the right bit and care.

  3. 03

    Mount the button

    Seat the button in the hole and lock it from below with the nut.

  4. 04

    Run the tube

    Route the tube down and keep it free of sharp bends or pinches.

  5. 05

    Connect the module

    Attach the tube to the module, mount the module and plug it into the outlet.

  6. 06

    Test

    Press the button with the water running and confirm the disposal starts and stops.

Always switch off power at the breaker or unplug the disposal before touching anything wired. Keep the module off the cabinet floor, where a small leak could reach it.

Drilling a countertop: the part that goes wrong

Laminate and solid-surface counters are forgiving. Granite, quartz and marble are not. A hole in the wrong spot, or a cracked slab, costs far more than the switch. If you have stone, ask the countertop installer, or hire a plumber who has drilled stone before.

Sinks with a pre-drilled air switch hole, or a hole left by a soap dispenser, make the job simple. Check before you drill.

Common air switch problems

If the switch does not work

  • ✓Check that the module is plugged in and the outlet has power
  • ✓Look for a pinched or kinked air tube
  • ✓Make sure the tube is fully seated at the button and the module
  • ✓Press the disposal's own reset button on the underside
  • ✓Test whether the disposal runs when plugged directly into the outlet

If the disposal runs on its own but the switch fails, the module or tube is the suspect. If the disposal also fails when plugged in directly, the problem is the disposal.

Plumber, electrician or do it yourself

A confident homeowner can install a plug-in air switch in about an hour. A plumber is the safer choice when the counter is stone, when the disposal is hardwired or when you also want a new disposal installed. The plumbing installation page covers the fixtures we set.

Air switch versus a wall switch: choosing for your kitchen

A wall switch is wired into the house and needs no module. It is simple, but it puts a power switch near a sink, and some kitchens have no convenient spot for one. An air switch keeps the control on the counter, away from the wall, and avoids running new wiring.

The trade-off is one more part that can fail. The module has a sensor and a relay, and a kinked or disconnected tube will stop the switch from working. If you plan a remodel, ask your electrician or plumber whether the existing outlet can power the module, and whether a plug-in unit fits the budget.

Think about accessibility too. A large, easy-to-press button can help someone with limited hand strength, and a counter button is easier for children to reach than a wall switch, which is a reason to use the cap lock feature some kits offer. Choose the style that fits the people who use the kitchen every day.

Permits and licenses for kitchen work in Ballwin

The City of Ballwin says building permits are required for all kinds of construction and lists basement finishing, decks and additions among them. Its permit page does not mention small kitchen appliance work such as an air switch or a disposal swap, so the Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 is the place to ask.

If you hire someone, the city requires contractors in Ballwin to hold a Ballwin contractor license along with an active St. Louis County license in their trade. Ask to see both before work starts.

Kitchen projects in Manchester or Ellisville follow each city's own rules, so make that call there.
