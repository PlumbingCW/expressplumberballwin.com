All Drains in the House Clogged? A Ballwin, MO Homeowner Guide

When every fixture is slow or backing up, the main line is the suspect. Here is what to do.

One slow drain is a nuisance. Every drain in the house going slow at once is a different problem. It means the blockage is past the branch lines, in the main drain that carries all the wastewater from your home to the street.

This guide helps you confirm that, protect the house while you wait and understand what a plumber does. The sewer line cleaning page covers the clearing methods in detail, and our 24/7 line at (314) 461-6239 is open if water is already rising.

How to tell it is the main line

Signs of a main line clog

  • ✓Toilets, tubs and sinks are all slow at the same time
  • ✓Water backs up in a tub or floor drain when you flush or run the washer
  • ✓The lowest drain in the house, often in the basement, overflows first
  • ✓You hear gurgling from drains far from where water is running
  • ✓A sewage smell comes from a floor drain

Try a simple test. Run the kitchen sink and watch the nearest tub or floor drain. If water rises in the second fixture, the blockage is in the main. A single slow sink with a clean trap points elsewhere, as our drain cleaning guidance explains.

What to do right now

Stop using water. Every flush, shower or load of laundry sends more water into a line that cannot take it, and the overflow comes out at the lowest opening. Keep children and pets away from any standing water, since it carries bacteria.

Find your sewer cleanout, a capped pipe in the basement or yard. A plumber will use it. Do not open it yourself if water is high in the line, because the cap can release a surge of sewage.

What the plumber does

  1. 01

    Open the cleanout

    The plumber confirms the clog location and relieves the backup.

  2. 02

    Cable the line

    A motorized cable with a cutting head breaks up the blockage and pulls debris back.

  3. 03

    Run a camera

    A short inspection shows the cause and the condition of the pipe.

  4. 04

    Recommend next steps

    Options range from routine maintenance to jetting, lining or repair.

If roots are the cause, expect them to return. Hydro jetting scours the pipe and delays regrowth, and a sewer line repair fixes the joint that lets the roots in.

Keeping the main line clear

Flush only toilet paper. Wipes marked flushable do not break down like paper. Pour cooled grease into a container instead of the sink. If large trees stand between your house and the street, ask a plumber about a yearly cleaning, which is cheaper than an emergency visit.

Questions to ask the plumber before work starts

A main line visit is easier when you know what to ask. Ask where the plumber will access the line, whether a camera will be run after cleaning, and what the price covers. Ask what the camera showed and whether it recorded the video for you.

Ask what is likely to come back. Roots and grease return, while a flushed object does not. If the plumber expects the clog to return, ask for the options, such as a yearly cleaning, jetting or a repair, and ask for the price of each so you can compare them.

Finally, ask for the invoice to list the date, the work done and the location of the blockage by distance from the cleanout. That detail is exactly what a repair program or an insurance adjuster will want later.

Why timing matters

A main line that is partly blocked can fail fully at the worst moment, like a holiday dinner with a house full of guests. The first slow drain is a warning, not just an annoyance.

Acting early usually means a simpler cleaning rather than a backup cleanup. If you notice gurgling or slow flow in two fixtures, run the nearest tub or floor drain test and call before the line stops.

Ballwin's sewer lateral program and repeat root clogs

The City of Ballwin runs a Sewer Lateral Repair Program funded by a $28 annual fee on property tax bills, which voters approved in April 1999. The city says root problems qualify only when a lateral must be cleared more than once a year with documented history, and that a structural defect qualifies if cabling cannot permanently fix it.

Homeowners apply through the city's MyGov portal with a $150 application fee. The per-job cap is $4,500, and the city says special circumstances such as deep excavation may allow up to $7,500. The program does not cover the sewer main, which belongs to MSD, and it does not cover cabling or video inspection costs.

Save every invoice from your plumber. Residents of Ellisville and Manchester should ask their own city whether a similar program exists. These details are according to the City of Ballwin Sewer Lateral Repair Program page, so confirm current figures there.
