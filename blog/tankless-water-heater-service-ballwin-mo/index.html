Servicing a Tankless Water Heater in Ballwin, MO: Flush Guide

Why tankless heaters need a descaling flush, and what to expect from a service visit.

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so it never runs out and it takes up little space. The cost of that design is that every drop passes over a small heat exchanger, and minerals in the water coat it over time.

This guide explains how to tell a unit needs service, what a flush involves and what a plumber checks during a visit. If your unit has stopped altogether, the tankless water heater page covers repair options.

What scale does to a tankless unit

Calcium and magnesium come out of solution when water is heated. Inside a tankless heater they form a crust on the exchanger walls. The crust insulates the metal, narrows the passages and makes the unit work harder for the same hot water.

Early symptoms are subtle. Hot water takes longer to arrive, the temperature swings in the shower, or the unit makes a faint rumbling sound. Later the unit throws an error code about flow, temperature or the exhaust.

Signs your tankless heater is due for service

  • ✓Hot water output is weaker than it used to be
  • ✓The unit shows a recurring error code
  • ✓You hear rumbling, popping or kettle-like sounds
  • ✓The water temperature rises and falls during a shower
  • ✓More than a year has passed since the last flush

How a flush works

  1. 01

    Isolate the unit

    Power or gas is switched off and the service valves are closed to separate the heater from the house plumbing.

  2. 02

    Connect the pump

    A small pump and hoses link the unit to a bucket of descaling solution, usually white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved product.

  3. 03

    Circulate

    The solution runs through the heat exchanger for about an hour, which dissolves the scale.

  4. 04

    Rinse and clean

    The unit is flushed with clean water, the inlet filter is cleaned and the valves are reopened.

  5. 05

    Test

    The plumber restores power or gas, checks flow, temperature and the display for codes.

Service valves make flushing easy. If your unit was installed without them, a plumber can add a set at the same visit, which makes every future flush faster and cheaper.

What else a service visit covers

A good visit goes beyond the flush. The plumber cleans the inlet screen, checks the exhaust vent for blockage or loose joints, tests the gas pressure on a gas model and looks for leaks at connections. On electric units, the plumber checks the breaker size and the wiring condition.

Condensing models also have a condensate drain, which can clog and trigger a fault code. Clearing it is a quick fix if you catch it early.

When to call a plumber because a flush is not enough

A unit that has been neglected for years may have damaged exchangers that a flush cannot save. A plumber can tell you honestly whether to repair or replace. The water heater installation page covers new units, including gas line and venting checks.

A water softener upstream of the heater protects the exchanger. It is worth considering in a home with hard water, and the water softener page explains the options.

Keeping a service log and spotting trouble early

A short log makes tankless maintenance easy. Write down the date of each flush, the model number, any error codes and the incoming water temperature in winter. If hot water output drops a little more each year, the log shows the trend and tells a plumber where to look.

Pay attention to the seasons. Incoming water is colder in winter, so a tankless heater works harder and flow can feel weaker even when nothing is wrong. A drop that appears in January and fades in June is usually temperature. A drop that stays through the summer is more likely scale or a clogged inlet screen.

Finally, keep the area around the unit clear. Boxes against the exhaust or intake can restrict air, and a unit in a cold garage or basement corner may need freeze protection. A few minutes of attention each season saves a service call in the middle of a cold week.

Questions to ask a service provider

Ask whether the visit includes a flush, an inlet screen cleaning, a vent check and a combustion test on gas models. Ask whether the plumber records the readings. A clear checklist tells you what you are paying for.

Ballwin water hardness and your tankless heater

Missouri American Water's 2023 water quality report for its St. Louis County system lists total hardness ranging from 66 to 226 parts per million as calcium carbonate, depending on which treatment plant supplies the area. Dividing by 17.1 gives roughly 4 to 13 grains per gallon.

That range reaches into hard water. Where your home falls depends on the plant, so test your own tap with a kit, or ask the utility which plant serves your street. The higher your number, the more often a tankless exchanger needs a flush.
