Gas Water Heater Pilot Won't Stay Lit: A Ballwin, MO Guide

Why a gas water heater pilot keeps going out, what you can check safely and when to leave.

A pilot light that will not stay lit leaves you with cold water and a safety question. The pilot is a small flame that ignites the main burner, and a sensor shuts the gas if the flame goes out. When it keeps shutting off, something is wrong with the flame, the sensor or the air.

This guide explains the common causes, the checks that are safe for homeowners and the situations where you should leave the house. The water heater repair page covers what our plumbers do on site, and the 24/7 line is open at (314) 461-6239.

Gas smell first: leave, then call

If you smell gas, do not relight anything. Leave the house, avoid switches and phones inside, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Return only when the utility says it is safe. A gas smell near a water heater is an emergency, not a repair job.

Why the pilot goes out

Common causes

The thermocouple is the usual culprit. It is a small copper probe that sits in the pilot flame and generates a tiny current that holds the gas valve open. When it ages, it stops generating enough current, and the valve closes as soon as you release the button.

Safe checks before you call

Homeowner checks

  • ✓Read the lighting instructions printed on the tank and follow them exactly
  • ✓Hold the pilot button for the full time the label says, often about a minute
  • ✓Look at the flame color, since a steady blue flame is healthy and yellow or flickering is not
  • ✓Check whether the pilot goes out when a door slams or the clothes dryer runs
  • ✓Do not remove covers, bend parts or use tools on the gas control

If the pilot lights and stays lit but the water is still lukewarm, the problem is elsewhere. The thermostat portion of the gas control or sediment in the tank could be at fault.

Combustion air and the flue

A gas water heater needs air to burn and a clear flue to exhaust. A closet that has been sealed, a new door or a clogged vent can starve the burner. A backdraft can also pull exhaust into the room, which is a carbon monoxide risk. A working carbon monoxide alarm near the heater is a sensible safeguard.

Our plumbers check the flue draft on every gas heater call. If the vent has failed or the heater is past its life, water heater replacement is the safer call.

Repair or replace

A thermocouple or a pilot cleaning is a modest repair. A gas control valve costs more, and on an older tank the money may be better spent on a new unit. Rust, leaks at the base or a unit past ten years tilt toward replacement.

Carbon monoxide basics for a gas water heater

Gas appliances produce carbon monoxide when they burn fuel, and a healthy vent carries it outside. A blocked flue, a cracked vent connector or a sealed utility room can let it into the living space. Carbon monoxide has no smell, so an alarm is the only warning.

Place a carbon monoxide alarm on each floor and near sleeping areas, and test it monthly. Replace alarms on the schedule printed on the unit. If the alarm sounds, leave the house, call 911 and do not return until responders say it is safe.

Keep the area around the heater clear of boxes and chemicals. Do not store gasoline or paint thinner near a gas appliance, since vapors can ignite. A plumber checking the vent during a repair visit is a good moment to confirm the alarm is installed and working.

What a safe relight looks like

Follow the diagram on the tank, because models differ. In general you turn the gas control to the pilot setting, press and hold the button, light the pilot with the built-in igniter or a long lighter and keep holding for the time the label specifies. Then release slowly and turn the control to on.

If the pilot goes out when you release the button, stop. Repeated attempts waste gas and do not fix a worn sensor. Wait the time the label lists for gas to clear before any new attempt, and call a plumber if the same thing happens twice.

The short version

A pilot that will not stay lit usually points to a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a draft or a failing gas control. Never relight if you smell gas, never force parts, and keep a carbon monoxide alarm near the heater. Call a licensed plumber for gas work, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside for any gas smell.
