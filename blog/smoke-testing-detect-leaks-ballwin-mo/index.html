Smoke Machine to Detect Leaks: A Ballwin, MO Homeowner Guide

What a plumber's smoke machine can and cannot find, and the better tools for water leaks.

A smoke machine pumps harmless, visible vapor into a pipe system, and smoke escapes wherever the pipes leak. Plumbers use the method on drain, waste and vent lines, where a leak lets sewer gas into the house. It is a useful tool for one kind of problem and the wrong tool for another.

This guide explains what smoke testing finds, what it does not and how it fits with the other methods on the leak detection page. If you have water running now, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line first.

What smoke testing finds

Drain and vent pipes are not under pressure, so they do not spray when they crack. They leak gas. A smoke machine plugs the system at the roof vent or a cleanout, fills it with vapor and watches for wisps rising where they should not. A crack above a ceiling, a dry trap, a missing cleanout cap or a failed wax ring will all show.

Where smoke appearsWhat it meansTypical fix
Around a toilet baseFailed wax ring or loose flangeReset the toilet with a new ring
From a floor drainDry trap or damaged drainRefill the trap or repair the pipe
In a wall or ceilingCracked vent or drain pipeOpen a small section and replace

From a vent not on the roofDisconnected or broken ventReconnect or replace

Sewer smells are the usual reason to run a smoke test. A smell that comes and goes can be a dry floor drain, a bad seal or a cracked pipe, and smoke tells you which.

What smoke testing does not find, and when to call a plumber instead

Smoke does not find leaks in the water supply lines. Those pipes are full of pressurized water, not air, and they do not hold vapor. A wet ceiling, a rising bill or a meter that spins with all fixtures off needs a pressure test, acoustic listening or thermal imaging.

Better tools for water leaks

  • ✓A pressure test isolates a line and watches for a drop
  • ✓Acoustic listening picks up the hiss of escaping water through floors and walls
  • ✓Thermal imaging shows hot-line leaks as warm patches
  • ✓A moisture meter maps damp drywall and flooring
  • ✓A sewer camera looks inside a drain line

What a smoke test visit looks like

  1. 01

    Seal the openings

    The plumber plugs the system at the roof vent or a cleanout and fills the traps with water.

  2. 02

    Introduce the vapor

    The machine blows a non-toxic smoke into the system under low pressure.

  3. 03

    Walk the building

    Inside and out, the plumber watches for smoke at fixtures, walls, the roof and the yard.

  4. 04

    Mark and repair

    Leaks are marked, photographed and quoted, and repairs are planned.

Tell the occupants first. Smoke can alarm people and, in rare cases, set off a detector. Warn neighbors in a shared building so nobody calls the fire department.

Smoke versus a camera

A camera shows the inside of a buried lateral. Smoke shows leaks that let gas out. They often work together, and a sewer inspection pairs well with a smoke test when a smell persists and the cause is not obvious.

Most homeowners need neither tool until something is wrong. Know the signs, and call when a smell, a stain or a bill points to a problem.

Common sewer odor causes you can check first

Before calling for a smoke test, check the easy causes. A floor drain or a rarely used sink can dry out, which lets sewer gas rise through an empty trap. Pour a quart of water into each unused drain and add a spoonful of cooking oil to slow evaporation.

Look at the toilet base next. A wobbly toilet or a stain around the base suggests a failing wax ring. Check the cleanout caps in the basement or yard, since a missing or loose cap can let odor into the house.

If those checks do not solve the smell, or if it comes only in certain weather, call a plumber. Wind direction, humidity and a partly blocked vent can all affect where sewer gas goes, and a smoke test or camera inspection can find the cause when guessing cannot.

Safety notes for smoke testing

Use only equipment and fluid made for plumbing tests. Smoke from fireworks or fire products is not safe for pipes or lungs. Test with windows open and keep pets away from the work area.

Do not run a smoke test while someone is using the fixtures, since flushing can drive smoke out of the wrong places. Tell your insurance adjuster if a leak found by the test has caused damage, and keep the plumber's report.

Ballwin occupancy inspections and hidden plumbing leaks

The City of Ballwin says occupied buildings must be inspected and certified for compliance with the city's Housing Code before a new resident, tenant or business moves in, and that a new occupant needs an occupancy permit through its MyGov portal. A sewer smell or a visible leak is the sort of problem that can come up in that process.

If you are a landlord or a seller preparing for an inspection, fix known plumbing leaks beforehand and keep invoices. The Building Inspections Department answers questions at (636) 227-2129.

Owners in Manchester and Ellisville should check with their own city's building office, since each city sets its own inspection rules. These details are according to the City of Ballwin occupancy inspection page, so confirm current figures there.
