Sump Pump Battery Backup Installation in Ballwin, MO

How battery backup sump pumps work, how installation goes and what to maintain.

A sump pump fails at the worst time. Storms knock out power, and the same storm sends water into the pit. A battery backup gives the pit a second pump that runs without the grid, so the basement stays dry when the main pump cannot.

This guide explains how backups work, what an installation involves and what to maintain. If your main pump has already failed, use the sump pump repair page, which covers emergencies.

How a battery backup system works

A backup system adds a second pump, a float switch and a deep-cycle battery with a charger. The second pump sits in the same pit, a little higher than the main pump. If the water rises past the main pump because it failed or lost power, the backup float lifts and the second pump takes over.

Backups come as a standalone pump with a separate battery box, or as a combination unit that includes the main pump. The first is the common retrofit, since it keeps your existing pump in service.

What installation involves

  1. 01

    Check the pit

    The pit must be deep and wide enough for two pumps, and clear of debris.

  2. 02

    Fit the backup pump

    The backup is set in the pit and its discharge line is run to the existing outlet, with a check valve.

  3. 03

    Place the battery and charger

    The battery sits in a vented box on a shelf off the floor, with the charger on a protected outlet.

  4. 04

    Test

    The plumber lifts each float and fills the pit to confirm both pumps run.

The discharge pipe matters as much as the pump. A second pipe or a tee into the first line must use a check valve so water does not run backward into the pit. Both pipes should end outside, away from the foundation.

Size and runtime

A backup pump moves less water than a main pump, and a battery gives a limited runtime. If the pit fills fast, a longer-lasting battery or a second battery helps. Ask the plumber how many hours of continuous running the system will give at the flow you need.

Maintenance that keeps a backup ready

  • ✓Test both pumps every few months by pouring water into the pit
  • ✓Check the battery charger light and the water level in a flooded battery
  • ✓Replace the battery on the maker's schedule, often every few years
  • ✓Keep the pit clear of gravel, silt and debris
  • ✓Listen for alarms and replace failed alarm batteries

Water-powered versus battery

Water-powered backups use the pressure of the water line to move water out of the pit. They need no battery. They only work with adequate pressure, and they send water down the drain while running, so they suit places with municipal supply and no well. Check local rules for that discharge before you choose one.

If your basement has taken on water through a floor drain instead of the pit, a backup pump will not help. That is a sewer problem for sewer line cleaning. See basement waterproofing for long-term fixes.

Alarms, switches and the habit of testing

A backup that fails silently is worse than no backup, because you think you are protected. Choose a system with an audible alarm for high water, low battery and charger failure. Some systems also send a signal to a phone through a wireless module, which is helpful if the basement is rarely visited.

Float switches deserve attention. Tethered floats swing freely and need room in the pit, while vertical floats slide on a rod and take less space. A float that rubs the pit wall or catches on a pipe will not lift, and the pump will not start. Move the float by hand during each test and watch the pump respond.

Set a reminder to test twice a year, once before spring rains and once in autumn. Pour in enough water to raise each float, listen for both pumps and look at the discharge outside to confirm water flows. Five minutes of testing is cheap insurance against a flooded basement.

Costs that surprise people

The pump is often not the biggest cost. A quality deep-cycle battery, a vented box, a charger and the alarm all add up, and batteries need replacing every few years. Ask for a quote that includes the whole kit and the battery replacement schedule.

Ballwin and the Meramec: planning for storm outages

The U.S. Geological Survey has published flood inundation maps for the Meramec River at Valley Park and Fenton, two communities near our service area. They show how far the river can reach in a flood. A backup pump protects against groundwater and power loss, not river flooding.

The City of Ballwin also says finishing a basement requires a permit and that mechanical, electrical and plumbing permits are usually needed. A finished basement with a sump system should have the pump and any new circuit included in that permit scope.
