Burst Pipe Repair in Ballwin, MO: Steps Before the Plumber

The first ten minutes after a pipe bursts, and how to keep the damage small.

A burst pipe turns into a flood fast. The first ten minutes decide whether you mop up a puddle or replace a ceiling. This guide walks through what to do, in order, and when to stop doing it yourself and call a plumber.

If water is spraying right now, skip to the shutoff section, close the valve and call (314) 461-6239. Our line is staffed 24/7, and the burst pipe repair page explains what the plumber does once on site.

Step one: find and close the main shutoff

Every house has a main water valve. In many homes it sits near the water meter or at the point where the water line enters the house, often on a basement front wall. Closing it stops water to the whole house, which is the single most important move after a burst.

Ball valves have a lever and usually need only a quarter turn. Gate valves have a round wheel and close clockwise. If a valve is hard to turn, do not force it with a wrench until it breaks. Call the dispatcher, who can talk you through other options.

The first ten minutes

  • ✓Close the main water valve and confirm by opening a faucet to see that the flow stops
  • ✓Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the pressure in the pipes
  • ✓Switch off breakers for any room where water is near outlets or a ceiling is sagging
  • ✓Turn off the water heater power or gas so the tank does not run dry
  • ✓Move furniture and valuables out of the wet area and photograph the damage
  • ✓Call (314) 461-6239 and describe where the water is coming from

Why pipes burst, and where to look

Freezing is the best-known cause. Ice blocks a pipe, pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet, and the pipe splits. The split often shows only after the ice melts, which is why a house can seem fine at night and flood at noon.

Corrosion and joint failure are the other big causes. Old galvanized steel thins from the inside, and copper develops pinholes. Fittings behind walls can also loosen after years of pressure swings.

If you cannot see the leak but the meter keeps turning with every fixture off, the pipe may be hidden. A leak detection visit can find it with listening gear and thermal imaging instead of tearing out walls.

What a repair looks like

  1. 01

    Isolate the section

    The plumber closes zone valves so the rest of the house keeps water where possible.

  2. 02

    Open a small access point

    A hole is cut at the leak. Moisture readings show how far the water traveled.

  3. 03

    Replace the damaged pipe

    The split section is cut out and new pipe goes in with proper fittings.

  4. 04

    Test and advise

    The line is pressurized and checked, and you get advice on drying and insurance records.

If the same line has failed twice, ask about replacing a longer run. The repiping page explains when that is cheaper than a third repair.

Drying matters as much as the pipe

Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold within a day or two. Remove soaked carpet pad, run fans and a dehumidifier, and cut away drywall that stays wet. Water removal and drying are separate trades from plumbing, so ask the plumber for a recommendation if you need one.

Photograph everything before you clean, keep damaged materials until your adjuster has seen them and save the plumber's invoice. Sudden bursts are commonly covered for the resulting damage, but slow leaks often are not, so ask your insurer how your policy treats each.

Prevent the next one

Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots before a freeze. Know where your shutoff is and make sure everyone in the house does too. A pressure gauge on a hose bib will show whether your house pressure runs high, since high pressure strains old pipe.

Ballwin burst pipe notes from the water utility

Missouri American Water, the utility that serves much of St. Louis County, publishes cold-weather advice for its customers. It tells homeowners to find the main shutoff, which is often near the meter or where the pipe enters the house, and to hang a tag on it so anyone can find it in an emergency.

The utility also says to close the main before thawing a frozen pipe, to warm the air around the pipe instead of using open flame, and to turn the water back on slowly and check for leaks. If you are away in winter, it suggests setting the thermostat to at least 55 degrees. Its 24/7 emergency line is 1 (866) 269-2837.
