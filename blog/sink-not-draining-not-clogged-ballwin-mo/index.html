Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged? Ballwin, MO Guide

What to check when a sink drains slowly even though there is no clog in the trap.

You cleared the trap. You ran a snake through the tailpiece. The sink still drains like it is swallowing through a straw. That pattern points away from the sink and toward the vent, the branch line or the main.

This guide walks through the checks in order, from cheapest to most involved. If the sink backs up now, switch to the drain cleaning page and call the 24/7 line.

Start with the obvious and rule it out

Quick checks

  • ✓Pull the stopper and clean it, since hair and soap hold up slow drains
  • ✓Remove and clean the P-trap under the sink
  • ✓Check whether the dishwasher or disposal shares the same drain
  • ✓Run the tub and a toilet to see if other fixtures are slow
  • ✓Listen for gurgling when water drains

If only this sink is slow and the trap is clean, the clog is further down the branch line or the vent is blocked. If other fixtures are slow too, you are looking at the main.

Blocked vents slow drains too

Drains need air. A vent pipe through the roof lets air in behind the water, so it flows freely. When a vent clogs with leaves, a nest or ice, the water drains slowly and the trap gurgles as air pulls through it.

A plumber can check the vent from the roof or by running a camera. Cleaning it is usually quick. Do not climb onto a roof unless you are comfortable, since winter and wet shingles are slippery.

A grease coat looks like a clog but is not one

Kitchen drains build a layer of grease and soap on the pipe wall. The pipe narrows without ever stopping, so a snake pokes a hole through and leaves the coating. Flow improves for a day and then fades again.

A cable cannot remove that coating well. Hydro jetting washes the whole pipe wall and often fixes this kind of slow drain for good. Pouring grease down the sink starts the cycle again, so wipe pans with a paper towel.

When the main line is the cause

A main line that is partly blocked drains slowly everywhere. The signs are gurgling in tubs and toilets, a floor drain that bubbles when the washer runs and slow flow in the lowest fixtures first. Roots, a sag in the pipe or flushed wipes are the usual causes.

A camera inspection shows the cause. The sewer line cleaning page explains how a plumber clears the main and how to keep it clear.

What not to do

Skip chemical drain cleaners. They rarely clear a coated pipe, they sit in the line and they are hazardous to the plumber who has to work on it. A plunger and a hand auger are fine for a single sink. Beyond that, call.

A short routine to keep sinks draining

Prevention is boring but effective. Run hot water for half a minute after washing dishes to move soap and grease along. Use a drain strainer in the kitchen and the bathroom, and empty it into the trash. Keep coffee grounds, rice and eggshells out of the sink.

Once a month, pour a kettle of very hot, not boiling, water down the drain to soften buildup. Avoid pouring boiling water into PVC pipe or a porcelain sink, since extreme heat can damage them. A baking soda and vinegar rinse can freshen a drain, though it will not clear a real clog.

If a drain slows again soon after you clear it, pay attention to the pattern. Same drain, same speed, every few weeks points to a coated branch line. Several drains slowing together points to the main. Write down what you notice, because the pattern helps a plumber find the cause faster.

When to stop and call

If you have cleared the trap, tried a hand auger and the sink still drains poorly, the problem is deeper. Continuing to push a cable you cannot control can scratch or puncture older pipe. A plumber has the tools to reach the branch line and camera it.

Call right away if water backs up into a tub or floor drain, if you smell sewage, or if the problem affects the whole house.

The short version

Clean the trap, check the vent, look at whether other fixtures are slow and think about grease. One slow sink with a clean trap points to the branch line or the vent. Several slow fixtures point to the main. Skip chemical cleaners, and call a plumber when the problem moves beyond a single fixture.

Who owns the pipe in Ballwin when a drain problem is not the sink

The Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District states that property owners are responsible for the entire private building sewer, which runs from the house to the public main. MSD also reports that about 85 percent of sewer backups are not caused by the public sewer.

If several neighbors on your street are slow or backing up at the same time, the public main may be involved. MSD takes reports at 1 (866) 281-5737. If only your house is affected, the cause is almost always the private line.

That rule holds for households in Ellisville, Manchester and Ballwin alike. These details are according to the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District guidance, so confirm current figures there.
