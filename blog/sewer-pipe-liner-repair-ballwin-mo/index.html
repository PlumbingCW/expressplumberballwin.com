Sewer Pipe Liner Repair: A Ballwin, MO Homeowner Guide

How a cured-in-place liner repairs a cracked sewer pipe without a trench, and where it falls short.

A cracked or rooted sewer pipe used to mean a trench across the yard. Pipe lining offers another path. A resin-soaked liner goes inside the old pipe and hardens into a new pipe within the old one, so most of the work happens through a small access point.

This guide explains how lining works, when it fits and when it does not. The sewer line repair page compares lining with spot repair and replacement, and our plumbers explain which one suits a given camera finding.

How lining works

  1. 01

    Clean the pipe

    Roots, scale and debris are removed with a cable or a jetter so the liner can sit tight against the wall.

  2. 02

    Camera check

    A camera confirms the pipe is continuous, sized and shaped for a liner.

  3. 03

    Insert the liner

    A felt tube coated in epoxy resin is pulled or inverted into the pipe.

  4. 04

    Cure

    Steam, hot water or light cures the resin, which takes a few hours.

  5. 05

    Reopen and inspect

    Lateral connections are reopened with a cutter and a final camera run records the finished pipe.

The liner seals cracks and joints, and it blocks roots from entering. It leaves a slightly smaller pipe, but its smooth wall usually flows as well as or better than the old pipe.

When lining fits, and when it does not

Lining suitability
ConditionLining works?Why
Cracks and open jointsYesThe liner seals them
Root intrusion at jointsYes, after cutting rootsThe liner blocks new roots
Light corrosion or scaleYesThe liner covers the rough wall
Collapsed pipeNoThe liner needs an open path
Large bellies or sagsUsually noA liner follows the low spot and holds water
Severe offset jointsSometimesDepends on the size of the offset

A camera inspection decides. The plumber measures pipe size, notes bellies and looks for collapses. If you are weighing options, ask for the video and ask why the plumber recommends lining, bursting or digging.

Questions to ask before you hire

Ask the contractor

  • ✓Does the quote include cleaning and a before and after camera video?
  • ✓What is the liner material, the length and the warranty?
  • ✓How are lateral connections reopened?
  • ✓Who pulls the permit and schedules the inspection?
  • ✓What happens if the camera finds a collapse mid-pipe?

Lining a pipe with a sag locks the sag in place. Be wary of a quote that skips the camera. A clean, dated video of the finished pipe is your record for resale and insurance.

Cost factors

Price depends on pipe length, diameter, access, the amount of cleaning needed and the number of connections to reopen. Lining a short section costs less than lining a long run, though a long run avoids a long trench. Ask for the price both ways.

A dig is still the right answer for a collapsed or heavily bellied pipe. Our hydro jetting service often comes first to clean the pipe so lining can go ahead.

Before and after: the paperwork to keep

Lining is a quiet repair, so documentation matters. Keep the before video, the cleaning invoice, the liner specification and the after video. Ask for the liner length, thickness and resin type to be written on the invoice, along with the warranty.

If you sell the house later, a buyer's inspector may run a camera through the lateral. Having your own recorded finish and warranty proves that the repair was done and what was done. Store copies in a folder with your home records.

Also note the location of cleanouts and any reopened connections. If a future plumber knows where the liner begins and ends, they can work on the line without surprises. Good records turn a one-time repair into a lasting asset.

Warning signs a liner may be needed

Slow drains that return after cleaning, repeated root cutting and a sewer smell near the yard are all hints. A camera visit turns hints into facts and shows whether the pipe is a candidate for lining or needs a different repair.

Do not wait for a full backup. A pipe with a small crack is far easier to line than one that has collapsed, and a planned repair costs less than an emergency dig in bad weather. Treat a repeated clog as information rather than a nuisance.

The short version

Lining repairs cracks, open joints and root entry without a long trench, but only in a pipe that is open and mostly straight. Get a camera inspection first, ask for the video, compare lining with spot repair and replacement, and confirm what any city program covers before you sign. Keep the before and after footage and the warranty with your home records.

How Ballwin's lateral program pays for repairs

Ballwin's Sewer Lateral Repair Program describes covered costs as excavating and repairing the failed section, backfilling and restoring the trench, hauling away removed material and patching driveways, sidewalks, curbs and pavement. The city does not say lining is covered, so ask the Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 before you sign a lining contract.

After approval, the city says the homeowner schedules the work with the program's contractor, the city, homeowner and contractor sign a contract and the contractor pulls the plumbing permit. If funds are available the contractor bills the city, and anything above the cap goes to the homeowner. If funds run out, the homeowner pays and is reimbursed up to the cap when money is available.

Homeowners in Manchester and Ellisville should check with their own city for similar help. These details are according to the program rules posted by the City of Ballwin, so confirm current figures there.
