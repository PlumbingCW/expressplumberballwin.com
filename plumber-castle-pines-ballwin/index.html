What is documented about Castle Pines

Sampled listings in Castle Pines show build years of 1995 and 1996, including a section labeled Castle Pines One-B, with two-story and colonial styles on lots of about 0.3 acre. Listings describe a community lake for fishing and a community pool with a swim team, and assign the area to the Rockwood School District, including Woerther Elementary, Selvidge Middle and Marquette High.

One sampled home had a partially finished basement with a sump pit and pump. As with any single listing, that is an example and not a rule for every house. The association's own website was not reachable when we researched this page, so we do not quote a home count.

The first water heater is now about thirty years old

This is general plumbing information, not a finding about any one house. Tank water heaters commonly last roughly 8 to 12 years. A heater that came with a house built in 1995 or 1996 is now around thirty years old, so most Castle Pines homes have had at least one replacement. If you have not replaced yours, or do not know its age, check the date on the label.

Signs that a tank is near the end include rusty hot water, popping from sediment, moisture around the base and a tank that no longer keeps up. See water heater repair, water heater replacement and tankless water heaters if you are comparing options.

Hard water in a 1990s house

Missouri American Water reported total hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate for the St. Louis County system in 2023. If that is your utility, hard water has been working on your heater and fixtures for as long as you have lived there. After three decades of use, scale in a heater tank and on fixtures is worth a look.

Who owns what

Under MSD's rules the owner is responsible for the private building sewer, and MSD is responsible for the public main it connects to. Call MSD at 1 (866) 281-5737 when a backup affects more than one house on your street.

A city program may help with a failed sewer lateral. The Sewer Lateral Repair Program has a $150 application fee and a $4,500 cap per job, and the city says it is aimed at continuous blockages that cabling cannot permanently correct. Inspections can confirm current rules at (636) 227-2129.

Call 811 before digging, including for a deck footing or a drain repair, so underground lines are marked. Rolling Hills Country Club Park, also in ZIP code 63021, has its own page: see plumbing in Rolling Hills.
