Disposal leaking under the sink: stop the water first

Switch the disposal off and unplug it or cut its breaker, because a wet unit and live power do not mix. Empty the cabinet, set a bucket under the leak and run no more water in that sink. If water keeps coming from the cabinet, close the main water shutoff and call.

Disposal humming or dead: safe checks first

Turn off the switch and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker before you reach into the sink. Never put your hand in a disposal. If the motor hums but the blades do not spin, something has jammed the flywheel.

Safe checks before you call

  • ✓Switch the disposal off and cut power at the plug or breaker
  • ✓Look for a hex socket at the bottom of the unit and turn it with an Allen key to free a jam
  • ✓Press the red reset button on the underside
  • ✓Check that the wall switch and the outlet have power
  • ✓Remove any visible item with tongs, never your fingers

What should and should not go down the disposal

Small food scraps are fine with cold water running. Grease, coffee grounds in bulk, potato peels, pasta and fibrous vegetables cause the clogs we see most often. Grease coats the pipe downstream, and what the disposal grinds today can become the kitchen line clog next month.

If your kitchen sink backs up often, the pipe past the disposal may be coated. Cleaning the line, covered in sewer line cleaning if the main is involved, solves the root cause.

Repair or replace

A disposal lasts roughly a decade with normal use, though that varies by horsepower and habits. A jam is a cheap repair. A burned-out motor or a cracked housing usually means replacement.

We install a new unit with a new gasket and discharge connection. An electrician is only needed if the outlet or wiring is not safe to use.
