Main line clogged: what to do while you wait

A main line clog shows up in more than one place. The lowest fixture in the house backs up first, which is usually a basement floor drain or shower. If you flush a toilet and water rises in the tub, the blockage is past the branch lines and in the main.

While you wait for the plumber

  • ✓Stop running water and hold off on laundry and the dishwasher
  • ✓Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line
  • ✓Locate your sewer cleanout, which is often a capped pipe in the basement or outside near the foundation
  • ✓Keep people and pets away from any standing water
  • ✓Photograph the backup if you plan to file a claim

How the main line gets cleared

The plumber opens the cleanout and sends a motorized cable with a cutting head down the line. The head breaks up grease, cuts roots and pulls debris back to the opening. When a cable only makes a narrow path, a jetter washes the whole pipe, as described on the hydro jetting page.

After clearing, a short camera run is the only way to confirm the line is open and sound. It also shows why the line clogged, which tells you whether to expect a repeat.

Public main or private line

MSD states that the owner is responsible for the whole private building sewer and that about 85 percent of backups are not caused by the public sewer. If your neighbors are also backing up, report it to MSD at 1 (866) 281-5737 while you call us. A private clog is the more common cause.

If the cleaning reveals roots or broken pipe, Ballwin's Sewer Lateral Repair Program may help with repair. The program lists a $4,500 cap per job and a $150 application fee, and the city counts roots that need clearing more than once a year among the qualifying problems. Keep your invoices and read the rules before you hire a repair crew.

Keeping the line clear afterward

Wipes marked flushable do not break down like paper, so keep them out of the toilet. Pour cooled cooking grease into a container instead of the sink. If large trees stand near the lateral, a yearly cleaning can be cheaper than an emergency visit, and a sewer inspection shows whether it is worth scheduling.

Standards note: The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for cleanout access on building sewers, which is why a plumber asks where yours is. The City of Ballwin lists the International Plumbing Code among its adopted codes, and the Building Inspections Department at (636) 227-2129 can confirm which edition applies.
