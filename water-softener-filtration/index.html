What hard water does to a house

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Heated, it leaves scale on elements and inside tanks. Cold, it leaves spots on glass and film on shower doors. Over years it narrows pipes, clogs aerators and shortens the life of water heaters and dishwashers.

Missouri American Water's 2023 water quality report for the St. Louis County system lists total hardness of 66 to 226 ppm as calcium carbonate, depending on the plant that supplies your area. That is roughly 4 to 13 grains per gallon, so some Ballwin homes see scale and others do not. A home test kit or a plumber's meter tells you your own number.

Softener, conditioner or filter, and what to do if one leaks

A salt-based softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium in a resin tank, then flushes the resin with brine on a schedule. It removes hardness. A salt-free conditioner changes how minerals behave and reduces scale buildup, but it does not remove them.

A whole-house filter handles a different job. A sediment filter catches particles, and a carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor. Missouri American Water treats its water, so many households choose a filter for taste rather than safety. If you have a specific contaminant concern, ask the utility for its report and test your tap.

Installation and service

A softener is plumbed in at the main, after the pressure regulator and before the water heater, with a bypass valve and a drain line for the brine. Outdoor spigots and a cold line for drinking water are often left unsoftened. The plumber sets the hardness and the regeneration schedule.

Maintenance is simple. Keep salt in the brine tank, clean the tank once a year and replace the resin when it stops softening, usually after many years. The plumbing installation page covers the other fixtures that usually go in at the same time.
